# Sauce: why our work reads drab and soulless, and the rules that fix it

On 2026-11-15 the owner looked at a run of correct, clean, well-lit work (a reel with real footage,
a themed UI kit, a detailed pinata on a green backdrop) and said: "it's all so drab and soulless just
like all the other things you have been making. Add sauce to 2D, 3D, video, all."

The diagnosis: everything was a CATALOGUE SHOT. One object, centred, on a flat colour, neutral face,
even light, nothing happening, nothing at stake. Correct and lifeless. The same scene redone as a
MOMENT (the pinata mid-dodge with a shocked face, the bat mid-swing with a smear, candy and confetti
exploding, bunting and balloons behind, low camera, warm key with a cool rim) was the fix. Every rule
below is a way of turning a catalogue shot into a moment.

## The five questions (answer them in writing before making any image, screen or shot)

1. **What is happening?** A verb, at its most extreme frame: mid-leap, the instant of impact, the
   moment before the fall, the reveal. Never "the object, standing there".
2. **Who feels what?** Every character has a readable emotion (brows, mouth, eyes, posture). Props
   can have one too (a wobbling tower is nervous). Neutral faces are banned.
3. **What is the joke or the tension?** One gag, contrast or stake per frame: the pinata panicking,
   the tiny thumb next to the bolt, the crown being snatched.
4. **Where does the eye go first, second, third?** One focal point (brightest, most saturated,
   sharpest), a secondary, and supporting life around them.
5. **What makes it feel alive?** At least three of: motion (smear, blur, trails, speed lines),
   particles (confetti, sparks, dust, petals), secondary action (balloons drifting, flags, hair),
   environment storytelling (a party behind, a crowd watching), sound in video.

## 3D and still images

- Stage a scene, not a backdrop: foreground, subject, background with 3+ story props. A flat cyc is
  only for technical model sheets.
- Camera: low (eye height of a small character, elevation 0-10 deg), close and wider (28-40 mm) for
  energy; tilt 3-8 deg for action. Dutch angles, foreground objects partly out of focus.
- Pose: push it 30% past what feels natural. Lean into motion, splay limbs, tilt heads. Asymmetry.
- Faces: brows (the single strongest emotion control), open mouths, eye highlights, pupils aimed at
  the action.
- Colour: one dominant hue family, a complementary accent, warm key light, cool rim, coloured bounce.
  Gradient sky, never a flat fill. Saturation on the subject, calmer around it.
- FX layer on every hero image: confetti, sparks, candy, dust, speed lines, a comic impact star, a
  smear of the fast object. Cheap to build, huge in effect.
- Detail where the eye lands, simplicity elsewhere (bkit.detail rules still apply).

## UI

- A UI kit is a costume for the game, not neutral chrome. Every theme carries the game's signature
  motif in its shapes: bunting-flag banners for a party, alphabet-block buttons for toys, lily-pad
  panels for a pond. Plain rounded rectangles alone are drab.
- Add illustrated life: a mascot peeking from panel corners or reacting on the result screen, stickers
  and badges set at slight angles (-4 to 6 deg), patterned panel backgrounds at low contrast, icons
  drawn in the game's style (not only generic icon fonts).
- Every reward is a show: things fly, burst, count up, wobble, glow; a result screen is a celebration
  with confetti and a sting, not a table of numbers.
- Titles with character: chunky display type with outline and a slight rotation; numbers big.
- Idle life: shimmer on the primary button, a gentle bob on the mascot, a pulsing notification dot.

## Video

- Every shot is a moment with a reaction: the subject DOES something on each line, and something
  reacts (a crowd, a character, a needle, a number that jolts).
- Give the video a character or host even when faceless: a mascot, the crown that keeps being
  stolen, a recurring sidekick prop with an expression. Viewers follow characters, not facts.
- Punctuation every 1.5-3 s: punch-in zooms, whip pans, impact frames (1-2 frames of a flat colour
  or inverted silhouette), shake on big beats, freeze-frame with a label, sound effect on every
  visual hit.
- Comedy beats: an over-dramatic reaction, a sticker or emoji reaction (drawn, not stock emoji),
  a record-scratch freeze on the twist, a deadpan caption.
- Footage is graded: push contrast and saturation, warm/cool split, vignette, grain; never raw.
- The ending pays off the character: the crown lands, the loser sulks, the host winks.

## Review question before showing anyone

Put the frame (or a contact sheet) next to a top competitor's. Ask: "If both were in a feed, which
would a 12-year-old stop on, and why?" If the honest answer is theirs, it is not done. Name the
missing ingredient from the lists above and add it.

## From the YouTube study (November 2026)

Techniques taken from 21 studied talks and devlogs on Roblox design, retention, loops, UI, monetisation and fun.

- A purchase is a moment: item flies to its slot, the buyer's name flashes server-wide for rare items, others can react.
  Nothing is bought silently.
- Cap decoration: at most one pop-up per minute, two visible timers, one pulsing badge. More is slop.
